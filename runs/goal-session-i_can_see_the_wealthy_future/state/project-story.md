# Project story so far

Trendora is a local-first, research-only US stock scanner that, after the market closes each day, ranks the market from overall mood down to individual stocks and explains every score it shows — it never just says "buy this."

## How it has grown

It began from a deliberately empty starting line: the first step built nothing and simply fixed the plan and the one rule that governs everything — every number is worked out once and shown the same way on every page, so a score can never disagree with itself. The next step turned that into a real, openable app: a permanent left-hand menu, a dark data-focused layout, and an honest status badge showing whether the data engine is connected and how fresh its data is — all sitting on about five and a half years of real daily price history for roughly 158 stocks and funds, frozen so the app runs entirely offline and answers the same way every restart.

Then the first numbers reached the screen. The Sectors page came alive, ranking every sector and industry fund strongest-to-weakest with an A-to-E grade, its strength versus the broader market, and its distance below its 12-month high — each row expandable to show the reasons behind the grade. The Dashboard appeared alongside it, reading the day's overall market mood as a labelled, scored figure with its drivers and the top sectors, drawn from the very same ranking the Sectors page uses.

The picture then filled out: the product began ranking every individual stock with three plain grades — how strong it is, whether it's at a good buy point, and how risky it is — plus a one-line reason and filters by sector and setup type; a new Themes page ranked investing themes like semiconductors and nuclear by price-confirmed strength; the Dashboard's last "pending" tiles turned into real figures (how many stocks are actionable today, and the leading themes); and a stock's grades were proven to read identically on every page.

This latest step gave each stock its own full page. Open any name — say NVDA — and you now see a price chart of daily candles with four moving-average trend lines and a volume bar beneath, the themes it belongs to as clickable tags, and a plain-language line stating the price where the idea would be wrong ("below the 50-day average at $198.73") — written by the engine, not the page, and honestly marked unavailable when a stock is too new to compute it. The three grades sit right alongside, still matching the ranked list exactly. Next, the product will keep a permanent, unchangeable record of each past daily scan, so you can reopen an earlier day — including a market downturn — and see exactly what it flagged at the time.

## What it can do today

The product lets users open a complete daily dashboard (market mood, breadth, top sectors and themes, how many stocks are actionable, and the data date); browse and filter a full ranked list of individual stocks, each with three explainable grades and a reason; open any stock's own page to study its price-and-trend chart, the themes it belongs to, the price level where the idea would be wrong, and what drove each grade; rank investing themes; rank every sector and industry; and rely on every score reading the same on every page. A permanent history of past scans and a watchlist are still to come.
