# Project story so far

Trendora is a local-first, research-only US stock scanner that, after the market closes, ranks the market from overall mood down to individual stocks and explains every score it shows — it never just says "buy this."

## How it has grown

It began from a deliberately empty starting line, fixing the plan and the one rule that governs everything: every number is worked out once and shown the same way on every page, so a score can never disagree with itself. It then became a real, openable app — a permanent left-hand menu, a dark data-focused layout, and an honest "is-the-data-fresh" badge — all sitting on about five and a half years of real daily price history for roughly 158 stocks and funds, frozen so it runs entirely offline and answers the same way every restart.

Then the numbers reached the screen: a Sectors page ranked every sector and industry fund A-to-E, and a Dashboard read the day's overall market mood from that same ranking. The picture filled out — every stock got three plain grades (strength, buy-point quality, risk) with a one-line reason and filters, a Themes page ranked investing themes like semiconductors and nuclear, the Dashboard's last placeholder tiles became real figures, and a stock's grades were proven to read identically everywhere. Then each stock got its own full page: a candle chart with moving-average trend lines and a volume bar, its themes as clickable tags, and a plain-language line stating the price where the idea would be wrong — written by the engine, not the page.

This latest step gave Trendora its memory. It now keeps a permanent, unchangeable record of every past daily scan. The new Scanner Runs page lets you browse a history of dated snapshots and reopen any earlier day — including real market-downturn days — to see exactly what the scanner flagged at the time. On those downturn days it correctly flagged zero stocks as worth acting on, everything watchlist-only; and any two days show genuinely different rankings, proving each snapshot is a true frozen record, not today's numbers in disguise. Next, the product will start grading its own track record — measuring how the stocks it flagged actually went on to perform.

## What it can do today

The product lets users open a daily dashboard (market mood, breadth, top sectors and themes, how many stocks are actionable, and the data date); browse and filter a ranked list of stocks, each with three explainable grades and a reason; open any stock's own page for its price-and-trend chart, its themes, and the price level where the idea would be wrong; rank investing themes and every sector and industry; rely on every score reading the same on every page; and browse a permanent history of past scans, reopening any earlier day exactly as it was. A self-grading track record and a watchlist are still to come.
