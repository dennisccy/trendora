# Project story so far

Trendora is a research tool for a stock-market investor, opening each evening with a short, honest briefing instead of a plain dashboard.

## How it has grown

Early rounds gave every stock a real sector label, built the evening briefing, locked each session into a tamper-evident record, and recovered 585 of 587 stocks lost in a recovery drill — until a deeper data problem forced the owner to switch the whole app off. A five-stage repair rebuilt the damaged data and re-verified it live; a testing-tool bug that had briefly touched the real protected data was found and fixed, and the app's memory footprint was checked and improved.

Iteration 26 proved the evening briefing's own promise: a saved briefing never changes, and its exported copy always matches what's on screen. Iteration 27 closed a related gap: if the data behind an old frozen briefing went missing, the page now says so honestly instead of quietly rebuilding it.

Iteration 28 rebuilt the home page. It is now called "Today" and reads top-to-bottom as a real ten-second briefing: market state, summary, what changed, leadership rotation, next-session focus, and the manifest strip. The whole old dashboard — every card it ever had, down to the remembered show/hide switches — moved intact to a new "Market" page, one click away from the sidebar. One new idea did not fully land yet: three small words meant to say whether the market is improving or getting worse showed "NA" everywhere, because they only get written into brand-new saved briefings and every saved briefing so far predated them.

Iteration 29 tested that idea for real. The team made one carefully chosen, permanently recorded request for a date the product had never looked at before — August 3rd, 2026 — and the three words appeared correctly: "improving", "improving", "little changed", agreeing with the plain-English sentence already on the page. But the page most people actually land on — today's own latest briefing, from August 12th — still showed "NA" for those same three words, because nobody had told the product to add them to that specific saved briefing yet.

Iteration 30 finished the job. The team made one more carefully authorized, permanently recorded update — this time to today's own briefing — and the three words now read "little changed" on the page a person actually lands on by default, matching the sentence right below them. The contradiction that had blocked this feature for two rounds running is gone, checked and re-checked against the stored numbers by hand. Two smaller, honest notes came along with the fix: updating today's briefing quietly removed an old warning that this day's data had once been lost and rebuilt after the earlier incident (both statements are true, but the warning is no longer shown anywhere for that day), and one of the automatic checks meant to guard a different, related feature was rewritten after it failed and has not been re-run since. Neither of these affects what a user can do today, and both are flagged for the owner and the next round to settle. What's left: two older, half-finished pieces of the Today page — a note on what changed since the previous session, and a plain-English daily summary — plus a standing question about how much memory the app needs on a small host.

## What it can do today

The product lets users see each stock's honest, mostly-complete sector label; see why each next-session candidate was picked and why others weren't; trust that each evening's saved briefing exactly matches what's on screen and never changes once saved; see the app honestly report when an old briefing's underlying data has gone missing or was rebuilt, rather than quietly hiding it; browse the two recovered trading days with corrected numbers, backed by checked-live repair work; read a reordered "Today" page covering market state, summary, what changed, leadership rotation, and next-session focus on one screen, now including three plain words — on the page you land on by default — that say whether the market is improving or getting worse; and reach the full former dashboard, unchanged, on a "Market" page reached from the sidebar.
