# Project story so far

Trendora ranks stocks with explainable, evidence-checked signals. This chapter tracks making the running app itself trustworthy day to day — fast, honest about its own state, and resilient under heavy background work.

## How it has grown

Early rounds removed a hidden backfill limit, moved slow calculations to run once at ingest, added a live status badge, fixed a cluster of low-memory crashes, and returned the team to a fuller review process, then proved the Regime Lab page can no longer crash under memory pressure and brought seven of the product's eight promises to fully passing after fixing a timezone mix-up. Iterations 62-66 spent five rounds chasing an elusive slow-reply pattern during heavy background jobs, rebuilding the checking tools themselves along the way, and eventually pinned down that roughly 7 in 100 health checks briefly stalled during one specific calculation step. Iterations 67-69 aimed increasingly precise instruments at that step, eventually naming the two exact pieces of code responsible — but the measurement kept getting worse along the way, culminating in the first-ever completely unanswered health checks.

Iteration 70 fixed it: instead of recalculating its own status from scratch on every single check, the app started preparing that answer ahead of time in the background. During a real 17-minute heavy data job, all 1,030 status checks came back quickly, the slowest under 1.3 seconds — the first zero-slow-answer round of the whole project. But the round's own test setup shut down partway through, so none of the eight promises could actually be re-checked that round, and a new risk was named honestly: if the background updater ever stalled, the cached status could in theory go frozen and stale without anyone noticing.

Iteration 71 closed that gap and did the re-check. The status-check system was hardened so it can never quietly serve a frozen answer forever — past a small time limit, it now redoes the work fresh instead. Then, for the first time in two rounds, the team re-tested all eight promises against the live app. Six came back clean: honest backfill-range handling, unlimited-range backfills, a truthful startup status, fast page loads across the whole site, instantly-served backtest results, and live disclosure of background number-crunching. But the re-check also caught something real: during a genuine 20-minute data job, the app's own status check went completely silent for two and a half minutes, traced to the database running out of spare connections. That problem — plus a nagging question over whether the test ran the normal, production-like way — is what the team chases next.

## What it can do today

The product lets users browse stock rankings, sector and theme views, backtests, and all five research tools, always with an honest status message while the backend starts up. Backfills accept any date range with no hidden cap and explain when there's nothing new to fetch. Backtest results and other aggregates load instantly from storage, pages fetch only what they need, and the app discloses when it's crunching numbers in the background. The status indicator can no longer silently go stale, and six of the product's eight core promises are now freshly confirmed working — though one, staying responsive during the heaviest data jobs, was found to genuinely break under load and is being actively investigated.
