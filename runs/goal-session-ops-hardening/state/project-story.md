# Project story so far

Trendora ranks stocks with explainable, evidence-checked signals. This chapter tracks making the running app itself trustworthy day to day — fast, honest about its own state, and resilient under heavy background work.

## How it has grown

Early rounds removed a hidden backfill limit, moved slow calculations to run once at ingest, added a live status badge, and fixed a cluster of low-memory crashes. Later rounds fixed a sometimes-silent status light, sped up two slow pages, stopped a busy data-fetch job from wrongly claiming there was no data, and returned to a fuller review process. Iteration 59 proved the Regime Lab page can no longer crash under memory pressure, and iterations 60-61 brought seven of the product's eight promises to fully passing after fixing a timezone mix-up that had wrongly flagged fresh data as stale.

Iteration 62 fixed the health report's latest-scan-date and stopped the Data Manager page erasing numbers on a single failed background refresh, then ran the project's first real end-to-end rehearsal of its trickiest promise — staying fast to respond during a genuine 15-minute background job. That rehearsal exposed cracks in the checking tools themselves. Iteration 63 fixed the restart-timing crack, but the count of slow replies during the heavy job rose for a reason nobody could yet explain. Iteration 64 fixed the practice-date problem for good — the check now picks its own fresh, unused day automatically instead of a person hand-picking one — and confirmed the slow-reply pattern was real and repeating: 59 of 930 checks were too slow, and for the first time one check got no answer at all.

Iteration 65 went looking for the exact cause and, honestly, did not find one: four separate measurements of the same heavy job, including a full real data job, found no place where the app pauses, so no product code changed. That round's own live check came back unusually clean (only 1 of 1,057 too slow), which only deepened the puzzle, since the same test has swung between clean and rough on unchanged code for several rounds running.

Iteration 66 dug into a different corner and found something real, just not what it went looking for. Profiling the specific step it was chartered to fix again turned up nothing to bound — a second honest empty result. But along the way, it fixed an actual bug: a background data-import job that gets killed and resumed used to log itself twice in the job history; it now correctly reuses one entry. It also built one shared stopwatch tool so every test measures the app's response speed the same way, ending the two-lane disagreement iteration 65 had flagged. That shared stopwatch delivered the worst news of the project so far: during one specific heavy calculation step, about 7 out of every 100 health checks now take longer than 2 seconds — even though the app never errors and never goes down. The team is re-aiming at that exact step next round, this time by watching the live app while it runs instead of testing the step in isolation. Seven of the product's eight promises pass fully; the eighth — staying fast to respond during heavy background work — stays partly done while the team closes in on the last gap.

## What it can do today

The product lets users browse stock rankings, sector and theme views, backtests, and all five research tools with an honest "starting up"/"backend unavailable" status. It accepts a backfill over any date range with no hidden cap, explains clearly when there's nothing new to fetch, serves backtest results instantly from storage, and discloses when it's crunching numbers in the background. Pages load quickly because they only fetch what they need. The Data Manager page keeps its snapshot and gap counts current on its own, and its job history no longer double-logs a job that gets interrupted and resumed. The Regime Lab page keeps working under heavy load. The app almost always answers its own health check quickly even during its biggest background job, though roughly 1 in 15 checks are still too slow during one specific calculation step — a promise the team is still closing out.
