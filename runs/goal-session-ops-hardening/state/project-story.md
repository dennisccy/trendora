# Project story so far

Trendora ranks stocks with explainable, evidence-checked signals — this chapter is about making the running app itself trustworthy day to day: fast, honest about its own state, and resilient under heavy background work.

## How it has grown

Early rounds removed a hidden backfill limit, moved slow calculations to run once at ingest instead of live, added a live status badge, fixed a cluster of low-memory crashes, and cut background-refresh memory 71% with identical numbers. Later rounds proved a deliberately-induced memory failure degrades cleanly, fixed slow price-loading, repaired a broken automatic recheck, and — after the owner raised the app's protective memory limit — found and fixed a slow bookkeeping step that re-scanned 26 years of stock-eligibility history on every new day of data, though the app went dark for about 42 minutes while proving it. The round after that bounded two more memory-hungry spots and went a full round without running out of memory even under heavy load.

Iteration 47 fixed the Evidence page's own freeze — over two and a half minutes down to about a hundredth of a second, with the underlying numbers proven unchanged — but its own habit of re-checking all eight core capabilities kept slipping, running too early and never repeating after later code changes landed.

Iteration 48 fixed the exact stuck step in bringing an old, missing day of price history into the app — proven under 25 seconds on three real runs — but uncovered two other slow cleanup steps sitting right behind it, so the whole job still didn't reliably finish end to end. For the first time, the automatic re-check confirmed two capabilities — accepting a backfill over any date range, and honestly explaining when nothing new needed fetching — against real, checkable job records rather than page text alone.

Iteration 49 bounded those two remaining slow cleanup steps: a historical backfill now genuinely finishes inside its 20-minute promise, proven on three separate real runs. But finishing that check surfaced a serious new problem — opening the Factor Lab research page while that same backfill was finishing crashed the whole app for nearly 13 minutes, during the round's own testing.

This latest round, iteration 50, went after that crash directly. It re-engineered the Factor Lab page's memory use at the exact spot that had been crashing, cutting its peak footprint from about 7.8 GB to about 3.1 GB, and taught it to fail one number at a time instead of taking the whole app down. It also stopped the app's own background housekeeping from competing with that page for memory at the same time. A real test — a data update running for 25 minutes while someone repeatedly loaded the Factor Lab page — came back clean: no crash, and the page kept returning correct, complete results throughout. But during that same round of testing, the app went completely silent (though still technically running) for 17 and a half minutes and needed a restart — a different, still-unexplained failure the team is now instrumented to catch and diagnose next time it happens. One promise also still isn't met: while the app is busy with heavy background work, its own "are you alive" check sometimes answers slowly, a few seconds late rather than not at all. Next: get that heavy calculation off the path that answers live requests entirely, then re-check all eight core capabilities against a fully healthy app.

## What it can do today

The product lets users browse stock rankings, sector and theme views, backtests, and all five research tools. It accepts a backfill request over any date range and honestly explains when nothing new needed fetching, with no hidden cap on how large a range can be requested. It serves backtest results instantly from stored data, never making a user wait on a live recalculation. It discloses honestly when it is crunching numbers in the background instead of leaving a page looking broken. Bringing in a genuinely new, older day of price data now finishes reasonably fast and, as of this round, has been tested successfully while the research pages are being actively used at the same time — though the team is still confirming the app stays responsive under every kind of heavy background load.
