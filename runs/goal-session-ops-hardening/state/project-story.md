# Project story so far

Trendora ranks stocks with explainable, evidence-checked signals. This chapter tracks making the running app itself trustworthy day to day — fast, honest about its own state, and resilient under heavy background work.

## How it has grown

Early rounds removed a hidden backfill limit, moved slow calculations to run once at ingest, added a live status badge, fixed a cluster of low-memory crashes, restored a fuller review process, proved the Regime Lab page can no longer crash under memory pressure, and brought seven of the product's eight promises to fully passing after fixing a timezone mix-up. Iterations 62-66 spent five rounds chasing an elusive slow-reply pattern during heavy background jobs, eventually pinning down that roughly 7 in 100 health checks briefly stalled during one calculation step; iterations 67-69 aimed increasingly precise instruments at that step, but the measurement kept getting worse, culminating in the first-ever completely unanswered health checks.

Iteration 70 fixed the core idea: instead of recalculating its own status from scratch on every check, the app started preparing that answer ahead of time in the background, and during a real 17-minute heavy job all 1,030 status checks came back quickly — the first zero-slow-answer round of the project — though the round's own test setup died partway through, so none of the eight promises could actually be re-checked, and a new risk was named honestly: a stalled background updater could someday serve a frozen, stale answer without anyone noticing. Iteration 71 closed that gap — the status check can no longer quietly serve a frozen answer forever — and re-tested all eight promises live: six came back clean, but the re-check also caught something real: during a genuine 20-minute data job, the status check went completely silent for two and a half minutes, traced to the database running out of spare connections.

Iteration 72 fixed both root causes at once. The database's pool of spare connections was resized to comfortably cover what the app's server actually allows at once, and the app now refuses to even start if that math is ever wrong again. The status check itself was changed so it never pauses to recompute under load — it always answers instantly with its last known value, honestly labeled with its age. Re-tested on the correct, production-style launcher under the exact same heavy-job conditions that caused last round's outage, the app answered every single one of 1,315 health checks, the slowest taking under two seconds. The "aggregates calculated ahead of time" promise (J-05) is back to fully passing. The "never goes down under heavy load" promise (J-07) improved sharply but isn't fully closed yet: this round also let the app hold more than twice as many database connections at once, and nobody has yet measured what that costs in memory on a machine that has crashed from memory pressure before — that check is next.

## What it can do today

The product lets users browse stock rankings, sector and theme views, backtests, and all five research tools, always with an honest status message while the backend starts up. Backfills accept any date range with no hidden cap and explain when there's nothing new to fetch. Freshly calculated aggregates are ready right after a data import rather than computed on the fly, backtest results load instantly from storage, pages fetch only what they need, and the app discloses when it's crunching numbers in the background. The status indicator can no longer silently go stale, and seven of the product's eight core promises are now freshly confirmed working — the eighth, staying responsive during the very heaviest combined workloads, improved sharply this round and is one memory measurement away from being fully confirmed too.
