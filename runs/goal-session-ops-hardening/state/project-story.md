# Project story so far

Trendora ranks stocks with explainable, evidence-checked signals. This chapter tracks making the running app itself trustworthy day to day — fast, honest about its own state, and resilient under heavy background work.

## How it has grown

Early rounds removed a hidden backfill limit, moved slow calculations to run once at ingest, added a live status badge, fixed a cluster of low-memory crashes, restored a fuller review process, proved the Regime Lab page can no longer crash under memory pressure, and brought seven of the product's eight promises to fully passing after fixing a timezone mix-up. Iterations 62-66 chased an elusive slow-reply pattern during heavy background jobs; iterations 67-69 aimed increasingly precise instruments at it, culminating in the first-ever completely unanswered health checks.

Iteration 70 fixed the core idea: instead of recalculating its own status from scratch on every check, the app started preparing that answer ahead of time in the background, and during a real 17-minute heavy job every status check came back quickly — though the round's own test setup died partway through, and a new risk was named honestly: a stalled background updater could someday serve a frozen, stale answer without anyone noticing. Iteration 71 closed that gap and re-tested all eight promises live: six came back clean, but the re-check caught something real — during a genuine 20-minute data job, the status check went completely silent for two and a half minutes, traced to the database running out of spare connections.

Iteration 72 fixed both root causes at once. The database's pool of spare connections was resized to comfortably cover what the server allows at once, and the app now refuses to even start if that math is ever wrong again. The status check itself was changed so it never pauses to recompute under load — it always answers instantly with its last known value, honestly labeled with its age. Re-tested on the correct, production-style launcher under the same heavy-job conditions that caused the outage, the app answered every one of 1,315 health checks. The "aggregates calculated ahead of time" promise came back to fully passing — but resizing the connection pool also raised a new, unmeasured question: how much memory the app now uses when it holds more than twice as many database connections at once, on a machine with a documented history of memory crashes.

Iteration 73 set out to answer exactly that question, and, honestly, could not finish the job. The team tried four times to measure the app's peak memory use under realistic database load: three attempts were interrupted by other work competing for the same shared computer, and the fourth ran cleanly for 26 minutes but stopped just short of the heaviest part of the calculation. Rather than guess at a number, the round recorded what it actually found — a partial reading of about 2.3 GB out of the app's 8 GB allowance — and left the "never goes down under heavy load" promise exactly where it stood before: one measurement away from being fully confirmed. Nothing else about the app changed or broke this round; every other previously-confirmed promise remained confirmed, though two of them (the backtest-storage promise and the background-activity-disclosure promise) are currently resting on last round's evidence because the automated screenshot tool captured broken pages instead of fresh proof.

## What it can do today

The product lets users browse stock rankings, sector and theme views, backtests, and all five research tools, always with an honest status message while the backend starts up. Backfills accept any date range with no hidden cap and explain when there's nothing new to fetch. Freshly calculated aggregates are ready right after a data import rather than computed on the fly, backtest results load instantly from storage, pages fetch only what they need, and the app discloses when it's crunching numbers in the background. The status indicator can no longer silently go stale. Seven of the product's eight core promises are confirmed working; the eighth, staying responsive during the very heaviest combined workloads, is close but still waiting on one memory measurement to be fully confirmed.
