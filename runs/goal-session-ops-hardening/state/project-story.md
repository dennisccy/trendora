# Project story so far

Trendora ranks stocks with explainable, evidence-checked signals. This chapter tracks making the running app itself trustworthy day to day — fast, honest about its own state, and resilient under heavy background work.

## How it has grown

Early rounds removed a hidden backfill limit, moved slow calculations to run once at ingest, added a live status badge, fixed a cluster of low-memory crashes, and returned the team to a fuller review process. Iteration 59 proved the Regime Lab page can no longer crash under memory pressure, and iterations 60-61 brought seven of the product's eight promises to fully passing after fixing a timezone mix-up. Iteration 62 rehearsed the product's trickiest promise — staying fast to respond during a genuine 15-minute background job — and found cracks in the checking tools themselves; iterations 63-64 fixed those cracks and confirmed the slow-reply pattern was real. Iteration 65 measured the heavy job four different ways and, honestly, found no single place where the app pauses. Iteration 66 fixed a real double-logging bug and built one shared stopwatch every test now uses, which then delivered the worst news yet: about 7 in 100 health checks were briefly slow during one specific calculation step.

Iteration 67 changed tactics: rather than testing that step in isolation again, the team built a small hidden instrument inside the running app to watch a real health request as it actually happens, run during a genuine 18-minute background job plus a no-job control run. It found the app's own request-handling briefly slows during heavy background work — but that only explained about a tenth of the one slow reply seen that round. Most of the delay was still hiding inside the health check's own calculation work.

Iteration 68 aimed the same instrument at that hiding place, adding a stopwatch inside the health check's own body. For the first time, it named where most of a slow reply goes: about four-fifths of it. The team also finally ran a previously-skipped hour-long test file, and it passed cleanly (17 of 17).

Iteration 69 split that calculation into its three separate pieces — a database-read step, a readiness check, and a daily pre-flight check — and found the two checks, not the database reads, are what's slow: together they explain almost all of every slow reply this round (94% of it, the best result yet). But this round's own measurement also got worse, not better: the slow-reply rate jumped roughly eightfold, and for the first time ever, three health checks got no answer at all within five seconds during the app's busiest moment — even though the app itself never crashed or returned an error. The team now knows exactly which two pieces of its own code are the problem, and the next round's job is to stop redoing that work on every single check and instead serve it from an already-computed value, the way the rest of the app already works.

## What it can do today

The product lets users browse stock rankings, sector and theme views, backtests, and all five research tools, always with an honest status message while the backend starts up. Backfills accept any date range with no hidden cap and explain when there's nothing new to fetch. Backtest results and other aggregates load instantly from storage, pages fetch only what they need, and the app discloses when it's crunching numbers in the background. The Data Manager page stays current on its own, and the Regime Lab page holds up under heavy load. The app answers its own health check quickly almost all of the time even during its biggest background job; the team now knows exactly which two internal checks cause the occasional slow reply, though this latest round showed that reply can occasionally take much longer, or not come at all within five seconds, without the app itself ever going down.
