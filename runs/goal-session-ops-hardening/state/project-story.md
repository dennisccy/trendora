# Project story so far

Trendora ranks stocks with explainable, evidence-checked signals. This chapter tracks making the running app itself trustworthy day to day — fast, honest about its own state, and resilient under heavy background work.

## How it has grown

Early rounds removed a hidden backfill limit, moved slow calculations to run once at ingest, added a live status badge, and fixed a cluster of low-memory crashes. Iterations 51-58 fixed a sometimes-silent status light, sped up two slow pages, stopped a busy data-fetch job from wrongly claiming there was no data, and returned to a fuller review process after some write-ups stopped matching their own measurements.

Iteration 59 proved the Regime Lab page can no longer crash under memory pressure, and a real kill-and-restart test showed the Data page still shows correct saved numbers in under a second. Iteration 60 had the app's best day yet — an 18-minute data job with zero errors — but mis-read a Data page number as stale because of a timezone mix-up, which iteration 61 corrected. That let the "aggregates are precomputed at ingest" promise move to fully working, bringing seven of the product's eight promises to passing; the Data Manager page also began refreshing its own numbers automatically.

Iteration 62 shipped two small verified fixes — a real latest-scan-date in the health report, and the Data Manager page no longer erasing numbers on a single failed background refresh — and ran the project's first real end-to-end rehearsal of its trickiest promises, including a genuine 15-minute background job. That rehearsal also exposed two problems in the checking tools themselves: one that could falsely report a broken feature if it checked too soon after a restart, and a rehearsal test that used up its own practice date. The team chose to fix both before any new feature work.

Iteration 63 delivered that fix: the checking tools now wait for the app to genuinely finish restarting before testing it, and the rehearsal-date problem was fixed twice over (once by the build team, then again after the app's own testing used up the new date on the very same day). The round also made real, measured progress on the app's last open promise — staying fast to respond during a very long background job — cutting one measured slow reply from about 2.85 seconds down to about 2.42 seconds. That's not yet under the 2-second target, and this round the count of slow replies actually rose compared to last time, for a reason nobody has explained yet. Every one of the app's 983 health checks was still answered and it served zero errors all day — the app never went down, it just occasionally answered a little slowly. The team's next move is a controlled repeat of the same test with no code changes, to find out why the slow-reply count jumped, before doing any more speed work.

## What it can do today

The product lets users browse stock rankings, sector and theme views, backtests, and all five research tools with an honest "starting up"/"backend unavailable" status. It accepts a backfill over any date range with no hidden cap, explains clearly when there's nothing new to fetch, serves backtest results instantly from storage, and discloses when it's crunching numbers in the background. Pages load quickly because they only fetch what they need. The Data Manager page keeps its snapshot and gap counts current on its own and no longer wipes those numbers off screen on a single failed refresh. The Regime Lab page keeps working under heavy load, showing a plain "Unavailable" notice instead of a misleading zero. The app almost always answers its own health check quickly even during a heavy background job, though it can occasionally take a few seconds longer than the target during the busiest moments.
