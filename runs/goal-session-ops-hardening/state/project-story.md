# Project story so far

Trendora ranks stocks with explainable, evidence-checked signals. This chapter tracks making the running app itself trustworthy day to day — fast, honest about its own state, and resilient under heavy background work.

## How it has grown

Early rounds removed a hidden backfill limit, moved slow calculations to run once at ingest, added a live status badge, fixed a cluster of low-memory crashes, and returned the team to a fuller review process. Iteration 59 proved the Regime Lab page can no longer crash under memory pressure, and iterations 60-61 brought seven of the product's eight promises to fully passing after fixing a timezone mix-up. Iteration 62 rehearsed the product's trickiest promise — staying fast to respond during a genuine 15-minute background job — and found cracks in the checking tools themselves; iterations 63-64 fixed those cracks and confirmed the slow-reply pattern was real. Iteration 65 measured the heavy job four different ways and, honestly, found no single place where the app pauses. Iteration 66 fixed a real double-logging bug and built one shared stopwatch every test now uses, which then delivered the worst news yet: about 7 in 100 health checks were briefly slow during one specific calculation step.

Iteration 67 changed tactics: rather than testing that step in isolation again, the team built a small hidden instrument inside the running app to watch a real health request as it actually happens, run during a genuine 18-minute background job plus a no-job control run. It found the app's own request-handling briefly slows during heavy background work — but that only explained about a tenth of the one slow reply seen that round. Iteration 68 aimed the same instrument at the health check's own calculation work and, for the first time, named where most of a slow reply goes: about four-fifths of it. It also finally ran a previously-skipped hour-long test file, which passed cleanly.

Iteration 69 split that calculation into its three separate pieces — a database-read step, a readiness check, and a daily pre-flight check — and found the two checks, not the database reads, are what's slow: together they explained almost all of every slow reply that round. But the measurement also got worse: the slow-reply rate jumped roughly eightfold, and for the first time ever, three health checks got no answer at all within five seconds during the app's busiest moment, even though the app itself never crashed. The team now knew exactly which two pieces of its own code were the problem.

Iteration 70 fixed it. Instead of recalculating its own status from scratch on every single check, the app now prepares that answer ahead of time in the background and hands out the latest ready answer when asked. During a real 17-minute heavy data job, all 1,030 status checks came back quickly — the slowest took about 1.2 seconds — down from dozens of slow answers and three missed answers the round before. It's the first zero-slow-answer round in the whole project. The one catch: the round's own test setup shut itself down partway through, so the browser and replay checks for all eight promises never ran this time — nothing broke, they just weren't re-confirmed, and that re-check is next up. A small new risk was also named honestly: if the background updater ever stalls, the status cache could theoretically go stale without anyone noticing, so closing that gap rides along with the re-check.

## What it can do today

The product lets users browse stock rankings, sector and theme views, backtests, and all five research tools, always with an honest status message while the backend starts up. Backfills accept any date range with no hidden cap and explain when there's nothing new to fetch. Backtest results and other aggregates load instantly from storage, pages fetch only what they need, and the app discloses when it's crunching numbers in the background. The Data Manager page stays current on its own, and the Regime Lab page holds up under heavy load. The app's status indicator now answers instantly even during its biggest background job, closing the slow-reply problem the team spent the last several rounds tracking down — though this round's own verification pass didn't get to run, so a quick re-check is still owed before that win is fully banked.
