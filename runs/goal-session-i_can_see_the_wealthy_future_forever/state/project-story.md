# Project story so far

Trendora is a local-first, research-only US equity leadership scanner — a decision-support workstation that ranks the market after the close and earns the user's trust with forward-tested evidence rather than ever shouting "buy."

## How it has grown

Trendora arrived at this session already substantially built — a dense, dark analytical dashboard that ranks US stocks, sectors, and themes after the close around a core of skepticism: every score is explained, every idea shows what would prove it wrong, and the product tracks whether its own past picks actually worked. Early passes closed the last gaps in that original vision — putting the backtest screen on one shared date control, letting any forward-tested return open up into the stocks, sectors, and ranking tiers that drove it, adding a Data Manager that backfills a single date or a whole range without ever inventing numbers, and confirming that scores read identically across pages, a saved watchlist survives a full restart, and the main screens load fast from saved snapshots — completing all nineteen original must-have journeys and meeting the first goal.

With the product complete, the owner set a larger target: a next wave spanning a bigger universe, multiple chart timeframes, and a set of factor- and pattern-research labs. The first pass added its two lowest-risk pieces — a stock's chart now keeps drawing past a chosen past date all the way to today (the later stretch greyed out and clearly marked) while the scores still reflect only what was known then, and the backtest page now shows the real return each top sector, theme, and ranked stock delivered, with one control that flips every return between time horizons at once.

The plan to widen the watched universe from 122 names toward roughly 500 then stayed parked across two passes: the machinery is built and tested, but the one-time download of real price and market-value history it needs kept hitting a temporary outage of the free data source, and the team refused to fake it with invented numbers. So progress turned, exactly as planned, to the research tools that work on data already collected — and this latest pass delivered the first of them. The scanner now spots three chart patterns instead of one: alongside the original volatility-contraction pattern, it flags a pullback to a rising moving average and a flat-base breakout, each filterable on the stock list, badged with a plain-language explanation you can hover, defined in the glossary, and backed by a System Health panel showing how those names actually performed afterward — with honest sample sizes, never a made-up number. Next up are the factor-research labs, once the new research section's navigation is approved.

## What it can do today

The product lets users see the day's market overview at a glance; browse ranked lists of stocks, sectors, and themes and filter the stock list by sector, by setup, or by any of three chart patterns; open any stock for a plain-English scorecard — identical on the list and the detail page — and the price that would prove the idea wrong; rewind the whole product to any past day with one shared date control and watch a stock's chart continue past that day to reveal what happened next; read forward-tested evidence of how past picks performed, broken down by stock, sector, and ranking tier; save a watchlist that survives a restart; grow the dataset on demand by date or range; and look up every label and pattern in a plain-language glossary — always with honest "not enough data yet" marks instead of fabricated numbers.
