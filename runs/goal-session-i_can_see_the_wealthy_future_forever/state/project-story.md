# Project story so far

Trendora is a local-first, research-only US equity leadership scanner — a decision-support workstation that ranks the market after the close and earns the user's trust with forward-tested evidence rather than ever shouting "buy."

## How it has grown

Trendora arrived already substantially built — a dark dashboard ranking US stocks, sectors, and themes after the close, every score explained and every idea showing what would prove it wrong. Early passes closed the original gaps — one shared date control, click-through from any forward-tested return to the names behind it, a Data Manager that backfills without inventing numbers, identical scores across pages, a watchlist that survives a restart, and fast snapshot loads — completing all nineteen original must-have journeys.

The owner then set a larger target: a bigger universe, more chart timeframes, and research labs. Charts learned to keep drawing past a chosen date all the way to today while scores still reflected only what was known then. Widening the universe toward ~500 names stalled — the one-time download of real price history it needs kept hitting an outage of the free data source, and the team refused to fake it — so work turned to data already in hand: the scanner learned three chart patterns, then a Research area opened with a Factor Lab (later split by market mood, combined across signals, and extended to a volatility family) plus a Setup & Pattern Lab studying every past occurrence, and a synthesis capstone to travel from any lab finding to the filtered leaderboard and on to a stock's scorecard.

The owner returned and re-scoped the goal: the data-blocked universe items no longer halt the project, the historical track record was consolidated onto a rewindable Backtest page (System Health retired so everything sits under the single shared date control), and the Research area's multi-signal combination was made genuinely useful — blending the chosen signals into one reliably-populated ranking instead of the old "meets every signal" line that was almost always empty.

This final pass gave the Research area its own All-history ⟷ As-of-date switch. You can now read every research figure either across all history or rewound to a chosen past day, seeing only what was known by then — and at early dates the app honestly marks "not enough data yet" instead of inventing a number. Crucially, this stayed a mode, not a second calendar: the whole app still has exactly one shared date control. With that switch in place every buildable goal is met and the product is delivered; the only outstanding wishes — a ~500-name universe and intraday timeframes — remain honestly recorded as blocked on an unreachable data source, ready to heal the moment a feed is available.

## What it can do today

The product lets users see the day's market at a glance; browse ranked stocks, sectors, and themes and filter by sector, setup, or chart pattern via shareable links; open any stock for a plain-English scorecard identical on list and detail, plus the price that would prove the idea wrong; rewind the whole app to any past day with one shared date control and watch a chart continue past it; read forward-tested evidence by score grade, benchmark, and control group on the Backtest page as of any past date; explore the Research area to test whether a signal sorts future returns — by group, by market mood, as a populated multi-signal blend, and across a volatility family — switching every figure between all-history and a point-in-time as-of-date view; study any setup or pattern's full track record; travel from a finding to the names behind it and on to the scorecard; save a watchlist that survives a restart; grow the dataset by date; and look up every label in a plain-language glossary — always with honest "not enough data yet" marks.
