# Project story so far

Trendora is a local-first, research-only US equity leadership scanner — a decision-support workstation that ranks the market after the close and earns the user's trust with forward-tested evidence rather than ever shouting "buy."

## How it has grown

Trendora arrived already substantially built — a dark dashboard ranking US stocks, sectors, and themes after the close, every score explained and every idea showing what would prove it wrong. Early passes closed the original gaps — one shared date control, click-through from any forward-tested return to the names behind it, a Data Manager that backfills without inventing numbers, matching scores across pages, a restart-proof watchlist, and fast snapshot loads — finishing all nineteen original journeys.

The owner then asked for more: a bigger universe, extra chart timeframes, and research labs. Charts learned to keep drawing past a chosen date to today while scores still reflected only what was known then. Widening the universe toward ~500 names stalled when the one-time download of real price history kept hitting an outage of the free data source — so work turned to data already in hand: three new chart patterns, then a Research area. The Research section grew into a Factor Lab (split by market mood, blended across signals, extended to a volatility family), a Setup & Pattern Lab studying every past occurrence, and a synthesis capstone linking any finding to the filtered leaderboard and on to a stock's scorecard. A re-scope made the data-blocked items non-halting and gave Research its own all-history to as-of-date switch.

The owner then asked to bring in real market data: pick which source to import from (pasting a key only when one is needed, never saving it), run an import that survives a busy source by pausing and resuming, and grow the stock universe straight from the Data Manager. The import source picker arrived first; a key-leak bug was found and closed. Data imports then learned to run in visible batches — the job card shows "chunk 1 of 7," pauses cleanly in an amber "rate-limited — resumable" state, and resumes from the next un-fetched chunk even after a full server restart. The Expand-universe job was then wired up, offering a fourth job kind that screens the committed 548-name candidate pool and lists exactly who passed and who was omitted with the reason for each.

Iteration 24 turned the Data Manager from an import console into a full data-stewardship surface. The Coverage panel was reworked so that every aggregate figure — price history range, universe size, symbols count, trading days, snapshot dates, backfill gaps — now shows alongside a plain-language definition, and a single sentence distinguishes "universe" (the scored, screened names) from "symbols" (everything with stored bars, including ETFs and benchmark tickers). A per-symbol table lets the operator inspect every ticker and every universe member at a glance — date range, bar count, and whether it is thin or missing — sortable and filterable. A new "Remove imported data" panel completes the curation side: the operator picks a removal scope, reads a confirm-preview that lists exactly the user-added bars to be deleted, shows the committed-seed bars that are protected, and enumerates the dependent snapshots and forward returns that would cascade out — all before anything is touched. The committed seed can never be deleted from the UI. J-36 (coverage) is now fully passing; J-39 (removal) is built and source-proven but waiting for a clean browser capture to cross the finish line.

## What it can do today

The product lets users see the day's market at a glance; browse ranked stocks, sectors, and themes; open any stock for a plain-English scorecard matching the list; rewind the whole app to any past date; read forward-tested evidence on the Backtest page; explore Research labs by factor decile, market mood, signal blend, volatility family, event study, and all-history or point-in-time toggle; travel from any finding to the filtered leaderboard and on to a stock's detail; keep a restart-proof watchlist; read every label in the glossary; import from a selectable provider with a session-only key; run large imports in visible chunks that pause and resume; grow the universe via the Expand job; read a labelled coverage panel with a per-symbol table showing date range, bar count, and thin or missing flags; and preview exactly which user-added bars and derived snapshots will be removed before confirming a seed-safe deletion.
