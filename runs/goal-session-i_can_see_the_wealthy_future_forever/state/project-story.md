# Project story so far

Trendora is a local-first, research-only US equity leadership scanner — a decision-support workstation that ranks the market after the close and earns the user's trust with forward-tested evidence rather than ever shouting "buy."

## How it has grown

Trendora arrived already substantially built — a dense, dark dashboard that ranks US stocks, sectors, and themes after the close, with every score explained, every idea showing what would prove it wrong, and a habit of tracking whether its own past picks actually worked. Early passes closed the last gaps in that original vision: one shared date control across every screen, click-through from any forward-tested return to the names behind it, a Data Manager that backfills a date or a whole range without inventing numbers, scores that read identically across pages, a watchlist that survives a full restart, and fast loads from saved snapshots — completing all nineteen original must-have journeys.

The owner then set a larger target: a bigger universe, multiple chart timeframes, and a set of factor- and pattern-research labs. A stock's chart learned to keep drawing past a chosen past date all the way to today (the later stretch greyed out and clearly marked) while its scores still reflect only what was known then, and a backtest view began showing the real return each top sector, theme, and ranked stock delivered, with one control that flips every return between time horizons at once.

Widening the watched universe toward roughly 500 names then stalled across two passes: the machinery is built and tested, but the one-time download of real price and market-value history it needs kept hitting a temporary outage of the free data source — and the team refused to fake it. So work turned to tools that run on data already collected. The scanner learned to spot three chart patterns instead of one. Then a Research area opened: its Factor Lab lets you pick any of eight signals and a horizon and see, across all stored history, whether stocks with more of that signal actually earned higher returns — sorted into ten groups, each with its average and a downside-only risk-adjusted return, plus a score of how well the signal lines up with the future. The next pass broke that effectiveness down by market mood — whether a signal that looks useful overall really works in calm, narrow, choppy, or risk-off conditions. And this latest pass added the question the lab was built for: you can now combine two or three signals at once and read whether stocks that satisfy all of them together beat each signal alone and the whole market — with the combined group shown honestly as "not enough data" when it gets too thin. Next the Lab will add a volatility family of signals.

## What it can do today

The product lets users see the day's market at a glance; browse ranked stocks, sectors, and themes and filter the stock list by sector, setup, or any of three chart patterns; open any stock for a plain-English scorecard — identical on the list and detail page — plus the price that would prove the idea wrong; rewind the whole app to any past day with one shared date control and watch a chart continue past it; read forward-tested evidence of how past picks performed by stock, sector, and ranking tier; open the Research area's Factor Lab to test whether any of eight signals sorts future returns — by decile and rank correlation, raw and downside-risk-adjusted, split by market regime, and now combined two or three at a time; save a watchlist that survives a restart; grow the dataset by date or range; and look up every label and pattern in a plain-language glossary — always with honest "not enough data yet" marks instead of fabricated numbers.
