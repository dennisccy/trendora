# Project story so far

Trendora is a local-first, research-only US equity leadership scanner — a decision-support workstation that ranks the market after the close and earns the user's trust with forward-tested evidence rather than ever shouting "buy."

## How it has grown

Trendora arrived at this session already substantially built — a dense, dark analytical dashboard that ranks US stocks, sectors, and themes after the close around a core of skepticism: every score is explained, every idea shows what would prove it wrong, and the product tracks whether its own past picks actually worked. The early passes closed three gaps: the backtest screen moved onto the single shared date control so picking a date once holds everywhere; any forward-tested return became something you can open up into the individual stocks, sectors, and ranking tiers that drove it; a Data Manager arrived to backfill a single date or a whole range with a live progress bar while refusing to invent numbers on a failed fetch; and a closing pass confirmed scores read identically across pages, a saved watchlist survives a full restart, and the main screens load fast from saved snapshots — completing all nineteen original must-have journeys and meeting the first goal.

With the product complete, the owner set a larger target — a next wave spanning a bigger universe, multiple chart timeframes, and a set of factor- and pattern-research labs. The first pass of that wave added its two lowest-risk pieces: travel to a past date and a stock's chart now keeps drawing past that date all the way to today (the later stretch greyed out and marked) while the scores still reflect only what was known then; and the backtest page now shows the real return each top sector, theme, and ranked stock delivered, with one control that flips every return between time horizons at once.

The two most recent passes both aimed to widen the watched universe from 122 names toward roughly 500. The team built and tested all the machinery — a transparent, reproducible screen over a documented list of real index members — but the one-time download of real price and market-value history it depends on paused on a temporary outage of the free data source, twice in a row. Rather than invent any numbers, the team made a single polite check each time, saw the feed was down, and left the universe at its current size — deliberately keeping the new "how the universe is selected" panel hidden until a genuine screen has run. The plan now is to stop waiting on that download and turn to the research tools that work on data already collected — spotting more chart patterns beyond VCP and testing which factors actually drive returns — so progress continues no matter when the feed returns.

## What it can do today

The product lets users see the day's market overview at a glance; browse ranked lists of stocks, themes, and sectors and filter the stock list by sector, setup, or the VCP chart pattern; open any stock for an explained scorecard — identical on the list and its detail page — and the price that would invalidate the idea; revisit past scan days as recorded and move the whole product to any past day with one shared date control; read forward-tested evidence against the market and a fair random benchmark and break it down by stock, sector, and ranking tier; on a past date, watch the stock chart continue past that date to reveal what happened next (display-only); read the realized return each top sector, theme, and stock delivered at a chosen horizon on the backtest page; save a watchlist that survives a restart; grow the dataset on demand by date or range; and look up every label and pattern in a plain-language glossary — always with honest "not enough data yet" marks instead of fabricated numbers.
