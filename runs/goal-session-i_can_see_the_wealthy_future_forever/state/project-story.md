# Project story so far

Trendora is a local-first, research-only US equity leadership scanner — a decision-support workstation that ranks the market after the close and earns the user's trust with forward-tested evidence rather than ever shouting "buy."

## How it has grown

Trendora arrived at this session already substantially built — a dense, dark analytical dashboard that ranks US stocks, sectors, and themes after the close around a core of skepticism: every score is explained, every idea shows what would prove it wrong, and the product tracks whether its own past picks actually worked. Early passes closed the last gaps in that original vision — one shared date control across every screen, click-through from any forward-tested return to the stocks, sectors, and ranking tiers behind it, a Data Manager that backfills a single date or a whole range without ever inventing numbers, and proof that scores read identically across pages, a saved watchlist survives a full restart, and the main screens load fast from saved snapshots — completing all nineteen original must-have journeys and meeting the first goal.

With the product complete, the owner set a larger target: a bigger universe, multiple chart timeframes, and a set of factor- and pattern-research labs. An early pass let a stock's chart keep drawing past a chosen past date all the way to today (the later stretch greyed out and clearly marked) while the scores still reflect only what was known then, and added a backtest view of the real return each top sector, theme, and ranked stock delivered, with one control that flips every return between time horizons at once.

Widening the watched universe from 122 names toward roughly 500 then stalled across two passes: the machinery is built and tested, but the one-time download of real price and market-value history it needs kept hitting a temporary outage of the free data source — and the team refused to fake it with invented numbers. So progress turned, as planned, to tools that work on data already collected. First the scanner learned to spot three chart patterns instead of one. And this latest pass opened a brand-new Research area: its first tool, the Factor Lab, lets you pick any of eight signals — relative strength, moving-average trend, distance from a 52-week high, volatility, and more — and a time horizon, then see across all stored history whether stocks with more of that signal actually earned higher returns. It sorts them into ten groups, shows each group's average return plus a downside-only risk-adjusted version, and scores how well the signal lines up with future returns — always with honest "not enough data" marks. Next come more research tools: whether a signal works better in calm or stormy markets, and combining signals together.

## What it can do today

The product lets users see the day's market at a glance; browse ranked stocks, sectors, and themes and filter the stock list by sector, by setup, or by any of three chart patterns; open any stock for a plain-English scorecard — identical on the list and the detail page — plus the price that would prove the idea wrong; rewind the whole app to any past day with one shared date control and watch a chart continue past that day; read forward-tested evidence of how past picks performed by stock, sector, and ranking tier; open the new Research area's Factor Lab to test whether any of eight signals actually sorts future returns, by decile and rank correlation, raw and downside-risk-adjusted; save a watchlist that survives a restart; grow the dataset on demand by date or range; and look up every label and pattern in a plain-language glossary — always with honest "not enough data yet" marks instead of fabricated numbers.
