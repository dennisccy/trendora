# Project story so far

Trendora is a local-first, research-only US equity leadership scanner — a decision-support workstation that ranks the market after the close and earns the user's trust with forward-tested evidence rather than ever shouting "buy."

## How it has grown

Trendora arrived already substantially built — a dark dashboard ranking US stocks, sectors, and themes after the close, every score explained and every idea showing what would prove it wrong. Early passes closed the original gaps — one shared date control, click-through from any forward-tested return to the names behind it, a Data Manager that backfills without inventing numbers, matching scores across pages, a restart-proof watchlist, and fast snapshot loads — finishing all nineteen original journeys.

The owner then asked for more: a bigger universe, extra chart timeframes, and research labs. Charts learned to keep drawing past a chosen date to today while scores still reflected only what was known then. Widening the universe toward ~500 names stalled when the one-time download of real price history kept hitting an outage of the free data source — and the team refused to fake it — so work turned to data already in hand: three new chart patterns, then a Research area (a Factor Lab later split by market mood, blended across signals, and extended to a volatility family; a Setup & Pattern Lab studying every past occurrence; and a synthesis capstone linking any finding to the filtered leaderboard and on to a stock's scorecard). A re-scope made the data-blocked items non-halting and gave Research its own all-history to as-of-date switch.

The owner then asked to bring in real market data: pick which source to import from (pasting a key only when one is needed, never saving it), run an import that survives a busy source by pausing and resuming, and grow the stock universe straight from the Data Manager. The import source picker arrived first; a key-leak bug was found and closed — the key is now scrubbed at the point every error message is built. Data imports then learned to run in visible batches: the job card shows "chunk 1 of 7," advances as chunks complete, stops cleanly in an amber "rate-limited — resumable" state when a provider pushes back, and resumes from the next un-fetched chunk even after a full server restart.

Iteration 23 wired up the Expand-universe job. The Data Manager now offers a fourth job kind: "Expand universe." When it is selected, sources that cannot supply market-cap data (Alpha Vantage and Stooq) are shown greyed out with a plain-language reason; Yahoo, Tiingo, and Finnhub are selectable. The job screens the committed 548-name candidate pool against the config rules and, when it completes, the job card lists exactly who passed and who was omitted with the specific reason for each — never inventing a member. The live market-cap fetch is still blocked on this machine by the same external data-source outage that has affected the app before, so the universe count stays at 122 for now, but the full machinery is proven with a test data source and records the honest outcome when the real feed is unreachable. The next step is to confirm the end-to-end browser flow and then build the Coverage table so operators can see exactly which symbols have data.

## What it can do today

The product lets users see the day's market at a glance; browse ranked stocks, sectors, and themes filtered by sector, setup, or chart pattern; open any stock for a plain-English scorecard that matches the list; rewind the whole app to any past day with one shared date control; read forward-tested evidence by score grade, benchmark, and control group on the Backtest page; explore Research labs that test whether a signal sorts future returns — by factor decile, by market mood, as a multi-signal blend, and across a volatility family, each viewable all-history or rewound to a point in time; study any setup or pattern's full track record; travel from a finding to the names behind it and on to a stock's scorecard; keep a restart-proof watchlist; look up every label in a plain-language glossary — always with honest "not enough data yet" marks; choose which data provider to import from with a pasted key held for that run only; start a large import that runs in visible chunks, pauses gracefully on a rate limit, and resumes even after restarting the server; and pick "Expand universe" from the Data Manager to grow the scored universe from a committed candidate pool — with ineligible sources clearly marked and blocked.
