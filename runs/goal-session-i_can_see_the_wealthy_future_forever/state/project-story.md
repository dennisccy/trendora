# Project story so far

Trendora is a local-first, research-only US equity leadership scanner — a decision-support workstation that ranks the market after the close and earns the user's trust with forward-tested evidence rather than ever shouting "buy."

## How it has grown

Trendora arrived already substantially built — a dark dashboard ranking US stocks, sectors, and themes after the close, every score explained and every idea showing what would prove it wrong. Early passes closed the original gaps — one shared date control, click-through from any forward-tested return to the names behind it, a Data Manager that backfills without inventing numbers, matching scores across pages, a restart-proof watchlist, and fast snapshot loads — finishing all nineteen original journeys.

The owner then asked for more: a bigger universe, extra chart timeframes, and research labs. Charts learned to keep drawing past a chosen date to today while scores still reflected only what was known then. Widening the universe toward ~500 names stalled when the one-time download of real price history kept hitting an outage of the free data source — and the team refused to fake it — so work turned to data already in hand: three new chart patterns, then a Research area (a Factor Lab later split by market mood, combined across signals, and extended to a volatility family; a Setup & Pattern Lab studying every past occurrence; and a synthesis capstone linking any finding to the filtered leaderboard and on to a stock's scorecard). A re-scope then made the data-blocked items non-halting, consolidated the track record onto a rewindable Backtest page, and gave Research its own all-history ⟷ as-of-date switch — at which point every buildable goal was met and the product was briefly called delivered.

Then the owner returned with three fresh wishes, all about bringing in real market data yourself: pick which source to import from (pasting a key only when one is needed, and never saving it), run an import that survives a busy or rate-limited source by pausing and resuming exactly where it left off, and grow the stock universe straight from the Data Manager. A quick "we're done" pass almost closed the book — but a careful check caught that none of these three are built yet, so the project is open again. Everything that worked before was re-checked and still works; next is building those import tools (all testable offline), which finally hands the long-wished-for ~500-name universe the button it needs to happen.

## What it can do today

The product lets users see the day's market at a glance; browse ranked stocks, sectors, and themes and filter by sector, setup, or chart pattern via shareable links; open any stock for a plain-English scorecard identical on list and detail, plus the price that would prove the idea wrong; rewind the whole app to any past day with one shared date control and watch a chart keep drawing past it; read forward-tested evidence by score grade, benchmark, and control group on the Backtest page as of any past date; explore the Research area to test whether a signal sorts future returns — by group, by market mood, as a populated multi-signal blend, and across a volatility family, each viewable all-history or rewound to a point in time; study any setup or pattern's full track record; travel from a finding to the names behind it and on to the scorecard; keep a restart-proof watchlist; grow the dataset by date; and look up every label in a plain-language glossary — always with honest "not enough data yet" marks.
