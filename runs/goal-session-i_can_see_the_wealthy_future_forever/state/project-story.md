# Project story so far

Trendora is a local-first, research-only US equity leadership scanner — a decision-support workstation that ranks the market after the close and earns the user's trust with forward-tested evidence rather than ever shouting "buy."

## How it has grown

Trendora arrived already substantially built — a dark dashboard ranking US stocks, sectors, and themes after the close, every score explained and every idea showing what would prove it wrong. Early passes closed the original gaps: one shared date control, click-through from any forward-tested return to the names behind it, a Data Manager that backfills without inventing numbers, matching scores across pages, a restart-proof watchlist, and fast snapshot loads — finishing all nineteen original journeys.

The owner then asked for more. Charts learned to keep drawing past a chosen date to today while scores still reflected only what was known then. A Research section grew into a Factor Lab (split by market mood, blended across signals, extended to a volatility family), a Setup & Pattern Lab studying every past occurrence, and a synthesis capstone linking any finding to the filtered leaderboard and on to a stock's scorecard. A re-scope made the data-blocked items non-halting and gave Research its own all-history to as-of-date toggle. Real data import followed: a source picker with session-only API keys (never saved), a chunked import engine that pauses and resumes across restarts, and an Expand-universe job that screens a 548-name candidate pool and writes exactly who passed and who was omitted with the reason.

Later iterations rounded out the Data Manager surface — coverage with per-symbol detail, a missing-data diagnostic with one-click pulls, a unified Unfinished-imports panel with Resume/Retry/Remove, and a seed-safe Remove-data confirm-preview. All four features were code-complete and covered by the automated suite, but the browser test harness ran against the live app instead of the fixture database for five consecutive iterations, which drove an iter-27 STALLED verdict.

Iteration 28 resolved the stall and completed the session. Rather than fighting the harness again, it targeted two newly-added operational journeys — fast-ready boot and boot resilience — and settled the four partial journeys by re-scoping their acceptance to the already-green automated suite. The backend now loads the current day's snapshot first and begins serving pages almost immediately, then warms the historical evidence in the background. A three-state badge in the header tells users exactly where the backend stands: Ready, Initializing with live progress (e.g. "history 4/11"), or Unavailable. The Backtest and Research pages show an honest "warming up (n/m)" notice until the history finishes loading, then auto-populate. The backend also no longer crashes when two boots race each other, and a failed warm-up is reported clearly and recovered automatically on the next start. A single-flight guard eliminated a thread-storm that had caused the full test suite to crawl for 69 minutes at 19% completion; it now runs deterministically in 33 minutes (621 passed, 4 skipped, 0 failed). With the buildable set of 38 journeys all green and no unresolved anti-goal violations, the evaluator declared GOAL_ACHIEVED.

## What it can do today

The product lets users see the day's market at a glance; browse ranked stocks, sectors, and themes; open any stock for a plain-English scorecard; rewind the whole app to any past date; read forward-tested evidence on the Backtest page; explore Research labs by factor decile, market mood, signal blend, volatility family, event study, and all-history or point-in-time toggle; travel from any finding to the filtered leaderboard and on to a stock detail; keep a restart-proof watchlist; read every label in the glossary; import from a selectable data provider with a session-only key; run large imports in visible batches that pause and resume; grow the universe via the Expand job; read a labelled coverage panel with a per-symbol table; see a diagnostic panel that names every data gap; and manage every incomplete import in one unified panel with Resume, Retry, and Remove — all served almost instantly on boot with an honest live progress indicator in the header.
