# Project story so far

Trendora is a market-leadership ranking tool that shows traders which scoring signals have genuinely earned their confidence labels — through rigorous out-of-sample testing, not in-sample curve-fitting.

## How it has grown

Trendora's first eighteen iterations built a trust layer of honest "Proven"/"Not yet proven" badges and an auditable evidence ledger, deepened price history to thirty years, widened stock coverage, and survived two memory-crash scares in its background data jobs.

At iteration 28, the five oldest trading ideas were honestly re-tested on the deeper history and all still came up short — a clean, honest dead end. The owner accepted that outcome at iteration 29 and added nine new roadmap items: a statistics-budget page, a rejected-ideas graveyard, a daily health check, a live-data drift guard, and three risk/concentration views.

Iteration 30 built the roadmap's foundation: a pre-registration registry that refuses to test any new idea unless it was written down first. Iteration 31 added a browsable graveyard of every rejected idea, including for the first time its internal early-research rejections, though a "jump to this idea's record" link needed one more check before it could be signed off as fully working.

Iteration 32 closed that loose end — the link now correctly jumps to the matching record — and shipped the roadmap's third governance page: a certification-budget panel showing at a glance how many ideas have been tested, how strict the bar for the next one is, and how much testing room is left, so nothing can quietly spend a year's rigor unnoticed. Every check this round came back clean, with no regressions and nothing rejected. Next up: either a single daily health check for the whole platform, or a self-check auditing the testing process itself for bias.

## What it can do today

The product lets users browse a leaderboard of hundreds of companies with an honest "proven" or "not yet proven" status on every score, open a fully auditable evidence ledger for every trading idea ever tested, and view up to thirty years of price history plus decades of index and macro context for any stock — all while its data-refresh pipeline stays fast even on the heaviest job. Users can browse the complete history of every idea the system has registered, tested, or rejected, including its internal early-research track, with working links from a rejected idea to its original registration; the system refuses to test anything unregistered, and now also shows, at a glance, how much of its statistical testing budget has already been spent.
