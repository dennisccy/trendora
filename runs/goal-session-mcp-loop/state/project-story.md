# Project story so far

Trendora is a market-leadership ranking tool that shows traders which scoring signals have genuinely earned their confidence labels — through rigorous out-of-sample testing, not in-sample curve-fitting.

## How it has grown

Early iterations built a trust layer of honest "proven" / "not yet proven" labels and a fully auditable record of every idea tested, deepened price history to thirty years, widened stock coverage, and survived two scares where a heavy data job crashed the whole service (both fixed and re-verified). By iteration 29, its five oldest trading ideas had honestly failed re-testing on the deeper history, so the owner accepted that outcome and queued nine new features instead.

Iterations 30 through 34 built out that new list — a registry of every idea promised in advance for testing, a browsable graveyard of rejected ideas, a panel showing how much of the statistical testing budget has been used, and a single daily trust banner shown on every page — closing with a full re-check confirming it all still worked. Iteration 35 taught that trust banner to also watch for live prices silently disagreeing with the saved, validated history, naming the exact stock and dates when it happens. Iteration 36 added a self-check on whether the testing system's own checker can be trusted — honestly reporting the checker isn't perfectly tuned yet, and correctly catching its hardest "cheating data" trap test with a loud warning rather than hiding it. Iteration 37 was a dedicated clean-up round with no new features: a full re-check of everything built so far (22 working areas) plus a fresh, formal confirmation of two open trust checks from the round before — everything held with zero changes needed.

Iteration 38 added a new "Concentration X-ray" to the Watchlist page — a view showing which of a user's saved stocks actually move together, grouping them into visual clusters, and giving one headline number for how many genuinely independent bets the list represents, plus crowding breakdowns by sector, theme, and current signal. The feature itself is solidly built and tested: real production data confirmed its math to better than ten decimal digits, and thirteen of fifteen live browser checks passed cleanly. But the round's usual last-step double-check — confirming none of the other existing pages quietly broke — wasn't finished in time, so the round is on hold until that check runs; nothing appears to be broken, it simply hasn't been formally re-confirmed yet. Two more views remain on the roadmap after that: a per-stock risk card and a drawdown/dry-spell outlook.

## What it can do today

The product lets users browse a leaderboard of hundreds of companies with an honest "proven" or "not yet proven" status on every score, open a fully auditable record for any tested idea — including combined-factor and relative-strength ideas — and view up to thirty years of price history plus index and macro context, each clearly sourced. Users can browse every idea the system has planned, tested, or rejected, see how much of its statistical testing budget has been used, and check one shared trust banner on every page that also watches for silent changes in live data and for whether the testing system's own checker is itself trustworthy. On the Watchlist page, users can also see how concentrated their saved list really is — which names move together, how they cluster, and how many genuinely independent bets the list actually represents.
