# Project story so far

Trendora is a market-leadership ranking tool that shows traders which scoring signals have genuinely earned their confidence labels — through rigorous out-of-sample testing, not in-sample curve-fitting.

## How it has grown

Early iterations built a trust layer of honest "proven" / "not yet proven" labels, a fully auditable record of every idea tested, thirty years of price history, wider stock coverage, and survived two data-job crashes (both fixed and re-verified). By iteration 29 its five oldest trading ideas had honestly failed re-testing on the deeper history, so the owner accepted that outcome and queued nine new features instead.

Iterations 30 through 34 delivered that new list: a registry of every idea promised in advance for testing, a browsable graveyard of rejected ideas, a testing-budget panel, and a single daily trust banner shown on every page. Iteration 35 taught that banner to also catch live prices silently drifting from the saved, validated history. Iteration 36 added an honest self-check on whether the testing system's own checker can itself be trusted — including catching its hardest "cheating data" trap test with a loud warning rather than hiding it. Iteration 37 was a clean-up round confirming all 22 working areas built so far still held, with no new features.

Iteration 38 added a new "Concentration X-ray" to the Watchlist page — showing which of a user's saved stocks actually move together, grouping them into visual clusters, and giving one headline number for how many genuinely independent bets the list represents, plus crowding breakdowns by sector, theme, and current signal. Real production data confirmed the underlying math to better than ten decimal digits, and thirteen of fifteen live browser checks passed cleanly (the two skipped ones were independently covered by other tests). The round's routine last-step double-check — re-confirming that none of the other existing pages quietly broke — wasn't finished in time, so the round is on hold until that quick check runs; nothing appears to actually be broken. Two more views remain on the roadmap: a per-stock risk card and a drawdown/dry-spell outlook, after which every currently planned feature will be built.

## What it can do today

The product lets users browse a leaderboard of hundreds of companies with an honest "proven" or "not yet proven" status on every score, open a fully auditable record for any tested idea — including combined-factor and relative-strength ideas — and view up to thirty years of price history plus index and macro context, each clearly sourced. Users can browse every idea the system has planned, tested, or rejected, see how much of its statistical testing budget has been used, and check one shared trust banner on every page that also watches for silent changes in live data and for whether the testing system's own checker is itself trustworthy. On the Watchlist page, users can now also see how concentrated their saved list really is — which names move together, how they cluster, and how many genuinely independent bets the list actually represents, along with sector, theme, and setup crowding.
