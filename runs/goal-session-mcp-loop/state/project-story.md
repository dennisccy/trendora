# Project story so far

Trendora is a market-leadership ranking tool that shows traders which scoring signals have genuinely earned their confidence labels — through rigorous out-of-sample testing, not in-sample curve-fitting.

## How it has grown

Early iterations built a trust layer of honest "Proven"/"Not yet proven" badges and an auditable evidence ledger, deepened price history to thirty years, widened stock coverage, and survived two memory-crash scares in its data jobs. By iteration 29, its five oldest trading ideas had honestly failed re-testing on the deeper history, so the owner accepted that plateau and queued nine new roadmap items instead.

Iterations 30 through 34 then built that roadmap's governance cluster — a pre-registration registry, a browsable graveyard of rejected ideas, a certification-budget panel, and a daily trust status strip — closing with a dedicated verification pass confirming it all still worked.

Iteration 35 plugged a live-data drift guard into that trust strip: it quietly checks whether freshly-pulled prices agree with the trusted, saved history, naming the exact stock and dates if something was silently revised, and turning the strip cautionary site-wide until a clean refresh clears it.

Iteration 36 completed the governance cluster's final piece: a self-check on whether the testing system itself can be trusted. It honestly reported an imperfect calibration (8-in-100 false alarms vs. a 5-in-100 target) and correctly failed its hardest trap test on deliberately "cheating" data, showing a loud warning rather than hiding it. The new page passed every test thrown at it and is now fully counted as delivered, after a fresh double-check confirmed two already-working pages were still fine. All four governance surfaces — registry, graveyard, budget, and now this calibration check — are complete. A brief paperwork tidy-up is next, then three pieces remain: a watchlist concentration view and two per-stock risk views.

## What it can do today

The product lets users browse a leaderboard of hundreds of companies with an honest "proven" or "not yet proven" status on every score, open a fully auditable evidence ledger for any idea, and view up to thirty years of price history plus index and macro context. Users can browse every idea the system has registered, tested, or rejected, see how much of the statistical testing budget has been spent, check one shared trust strip on every page that also watches for silent changes in live data, and now see whether the testing system's own checker is itself trustworthy.
