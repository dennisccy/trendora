# Project story so far

Trendora is a market-leadership ranking tool that shows traders which scoring signals have genuinely earned their confidence labels — through rigorous out-of-sample testing, not in-sample curve-fitting.

## How it has grown

Trendora's first eighteen iterations built a trust layer of honest "Proven"/"Not yet proven" badges and an auditable evidence ledger, then deepened price history to thirty years and widened stock coverage, surviving and fixing two memory-crash scares in the background data jobs along the way.

At iteration 28, the five oldest trading ideas were honestly re-tested on the deeper history and all still came up short — a clean, honest dead end. The product owner accepted that outcome at iteration 29 and added nine new items to the roadmap: a statistics-budget page, a rejected-ideas graveyard, a daily health check, a live-data drift guard, and three risk/concentration views.

Iteration 30 built the roadmap's foundation: a pre-registration registry that refuses to test any brand-new idea unless it was written down first. Iteration 31 added a browsable graveyard of every idea the system has ever rejected, including for the first time its internal early-research rejections, though a "jump to this idea's record" link needed one more check before it could be signed off as fully working.

Iteration 32 closed that loose end — the link now correctly jumps straight to the matching record — and shipped the roadmap's third governance page: a certification-budget panel showing, at a glance, how many ideas have been tested, how strict the bar for the next one is, and how much testing room is left, so nothing can quietly spend a year's worth of rigor unnoticed. Every check this round came back clean, with no regressions and nothing rejected. Next up: either a single daily health check for the whole platform, or a self-check that audits the testing process itself for bias.

## What it can do today

The product lets users browse a leaderboard of hundreds of companies with an honest "proven" or "not yet proven" status on every score, open a fully auditable evidence ledger for every trading idea ever tested, and view up to thirty years of price history plus decades of index and macro context for any stock. The Data Manager page runs its heaviest data-refresh job reliably. Users can browse the complete history of every idea the system has registered, tested, or rejected — including its internal early-research track, with working links from a rejected idea to its original registration — and the system refuses to test anything that wasn't registered first. Users can also see, at a glance, how much of the platform's statistical testing budget has already been spent.
