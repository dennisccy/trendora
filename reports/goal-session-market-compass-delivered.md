# Delivered — Market Compass

**Session:** market-compass
**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Final verdict:** GOAL_ACHIEVED
**Iterations:** 41 (iter-0 through iter-40)

## What you can do today

Open the evening market briefing for any date across 30 years of history without it ever crashing. See each stock's honest sector label, filled in for nearly all stocks instead of leaving most of them unlabeled. See the true reason each candidate stock was picked — or skipped — for extra attention next session, including the names that came closest without quite qualifying. Read a plain-English daily summary that sticks strictly to what the numbers actually show, with the source facts one click away. See a complete "what changed since last time" list that now accounts for every stock move that crossed the bar, not just the handful shown on screen, and honestly reports how many more were held back. See which sectors and market themes are gaining ground and which are losing it, in both directions. Visit the Market page for the complete historical dashboard, unchanged and intact. Trust that once an evening's briefing is saved, it never quietly changes afterward — and that the two trading days lost in an earlier data mishap were safely and verifiably restored. Underneath it all, the system now runs light enough to share the computer with other work without freezing it.

## How it came together

The project started by giving every stock an honest, complete sector label, building the foundations of the evening briefing, and making each saved session tamper-evident — a record that can be trusted never to have been quietly altered. Along the way the team weathered a data-recovery drill and a data-repair incident, both resolved without losing anything real.

The home page was then rebuilt from a plain dashboard into a genuine "Today" page — something a person can read in about ten seconds and understand what kind of market day it was. The old dashboard didn't disappear; it moved to its own "Market" page for anyone who wants to dig deeper. Soon after, the briefing gained trend words, a "what changed since last time" list, and a plain-English daily summary, and the team trimmed the program's memory use well under the computer's limit. The project was declared finished once, pending final confirmation.

The goal then grew to add honest "picked or skipped" explanations for every candidate stock and a new panel showing which sectors and themes are rotating into or out of favor. A hidden bug that was wrongly excluding qualified stocks got caught and fixed. The rotation panel took two attempts — the first came up short on proof, the second landed cleanly — and the project was declared finished a second time.

The goal grew once more, this time to make sure skipped candidates always got their true reason and that the "what changed" list never silently dropped a stock move it had already checked. The first attempt at this introduced a serious problem: almost every older saved evening started crashing instead of loading. The very next round tracked the issue down, fixed it, and personally re-verified every affected evening against the underlying saved data rather than taking anyone's word for it. A final round then closed the last gap — the "what changed" list now honestly accounts for every qualifying stock move, not just the ones shown on screen. With all fifteen promised capabilities verified working, the project was declared finished for a third and final time.
